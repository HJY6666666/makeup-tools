How to choose false lashes for your beauty brand

A good lash assortment begins with the person who will wear it. Before choosing a style name or a box design, define the look, comfort and occasion your customer is shopping for.

False lashes can be a focused category or one part of a wider beauty kit. Either way, a clear brief helps you compare product options on more than appearance alone. This guide gives you a practical starting point for that brief.

1. Start with the wearer and the occasion

Write one sentence about the customer and where the lash belongs in their routine. A soft everyday edit, a bridal-friendly choice and a high-impact evening collection may all use different lengths, density and band styles.

  • Everyday: lighter density and a shape that supports a natural finish.
  • Event or glam: more visible dimension, definition or length.
  • Starter collection: a small group of distinct looks that are easy to explain.

Reference photos are helpful when used as a direction, not as an instruction to copy another brand’s product. Describe the features you like: airy, full, winged, rounded, short or dramatic.

2. Compare the features that affect the finished look

Feature Why it matters in a brief
Shape Rounded and winged shapes can create different visual balance around the eye.
Length and density These guide how visible the lash feels in person and in photography.
Band style A clear or darker band changes the finished look and application preference.
Construction Ask how the lash is made and what product information is available for the specific option.
Packaging Tray format, outer box and label area affect the retail presentation.

Product naming and technical terms can vary between suppliers. Ask for actual product photos, measurements, material information and sample availability for the exact style you are considering.

3. Build a collection with a reason for each style

Rather than selecting several similar pairs, choose styles that have a different role. For example, a collection might include a subtle everyday style, a soft wispy option and a fuller statement lash. Your product pages and retail team can then explain the difference clearly.

If you are beginning with lash clusters or applicators too, keep the customer journey simple: what does the customer need to apply, wear and store the product comfortably?

4. Ask the questions that make a quote useful

Pricing depends on the actual product, quantity, packaging, samples and destination. A useful inquiry should include the category, desired style direction, target quantity, packaging needs and delivery country. You can also ask what minimum quantities apply to each option, rather than assuming one figure works for every item.
